# Getting Good at Prioritizing

Let’s say you just cleaned your desk at home, made a coffee, and are finally ready to finish the item on your to-do list that you have been working away at all week. Not even five minutes in, you get a text from a friend asking if you could help with an assignment. It is urgent, and you seem to be the only person capable of helping. What do you do?

We are the most overstimulated humans in history. Attention spans are at an all-time low, stress levels are at an all-time high, and screen times are through the roof. It is no surprise that prioritizing feels difficult. It is hard to pick which ball to catch when thousands of baseballs are being pitched at you at the same time.

The most successful people in the world are experts at prioritizing. Being an excellent prioritizer gives you the ability to execute quickly, waste less time in uncertainty, and get whatever needs to get done, done.

One of my goals a couple years ago was to become more time-efficient. I searched online, used a bunch of productivity apps, and tried countless methods, but I ended up more overwhelmed while trying to micromanage my life. What worked was the opposite of what I expected: disconnecting from everything and thinking about the biggest problem that needed to be solved.
