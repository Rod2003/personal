# Routines & Benefits

The best routines are the ones that are not too fragile and also not insignificant. By fragile I mean hard to follow or maintain; running a marathon every day, for example. If you run a marathon one day and try to run another one the next day completely exhausted, you might stop halfway. Still determined to keep the morning routine going, you might try running another marathon the day after and end up injured for a week.

On the other hand, a routine should not be totally insignificant. Re-organizing your closet every single morning can be a way to start the day on a positive note, but recurring activities feel most meaningful when they train discipline, check off necessary things, or build toward something over time.

Most people already have routines, such as a morning or nighttime routine. There are also routines that are less obvious, such as a routine before focused work or post-workout stretching.

I have found that some routines can waste time instead of helping in the way they seem to. I used to think I could only write in certain environments. The conditions had to be perfect: water bottle filled up, notifications checked off, no tabs open, the right music playing, and my desk perfectly organized. I eventually realized that having a pre-writing routine was halting my progress, since my goal from the start was simply to write quality work.
